# Editorial constitution: make the site sound like Ninad

This document governs public prose on ninadpathak.com. It exists because accurate information can still be lifeless, and lifeless writing makes Ninad interchangeable with the writer sitting one result above him.

## The voice source stays raw

Ninad's words are the source, including their heat and rough edges:

> "The main basis or fundamnetal is that tech documentation and developer content is an absolute necessity. Crack it and you've captured more market than anyone in your niche. Suck at it or try to automate it with AI and developers will smell it from so far away you'd not even see them running."

> "I want the full website to be opinionated as fuck."

Editing may correct spelling, tighten logic, or keep a claim inside its evidence. It must not replace `crack`, `suck`, `smell`, or `running` with bloodless abstractions such as optimization, friction, or noticing.

The public argument may add nuance around those lines. It may not use nuance to tranquilize them.

## The position

Technical documentation and developer content are how a developer learns whether a product deserves another ten minutes. A product can be sound and still lose the person trying it because the guide guesses, the reference hides the boundary, or the tutorial celebrates code nobody ran.

Teams that understand product behavior and teach it well give people a reason to trust the product and a path to adopt it. That can create a serious market advantage. It does not guarantee category leadership, and this site will not invent a statistic to make the conviction sound scientific.

AI belongs in research, preparation, testing, corpus inspection, and editing. It can speed up work that already has an accountable human judgment behind it. Asking AI to automate understanding produces prose that sounds finished before anyone knows whether it is true. Developers deserve better than that, and Ninad refuses to publish it.

## Heat is information

A revision fails when it preserves facts while removing heat, image, rhythm, humor, disgust, desire, or personal stake.

Strong opinions should sound like someone at a table who cares and has seen enough. They do not need aggressive adjectives. They need a target, a consequence, and a reason Ninad chose this position over the comfortable default.

Profanity stays when it is the sentence's natural pressure valve. Sprinkling it over generic copy makes both the profanity and the copy embarrassing.

## Every page needs a job and a fight

A public page must answer six questions:

1. What familiar situation, product behavior, or earlier practice lets the reader enter the page?
2. What contestable claim does Ninad make?
3. What default approach or opposing position is described fairly?
4. What evidence supports the claim, and where did that evidence come from?
5. Why does each section exist in the reader's path?
6. What decision should the reader make differently after reading?

The page fails the gate when any answer is boilerplate. It also fails when the prose shouts harder than the evidence permits.

## Evidence has a name

Claims may come from:

- a linked primary source
- a checked repository, fixture, command, or benchmark artifact
- a public portfolio item or a user-confirmed relationship
- a first-person experience Ninad has confirmed
- a clearly labeled opinion or inference

The only relationships confirmed for positioning copy in this audit are Semrush, Adobe, TinyFish, Mastra, Manicule, and OpenComputer. Existing attributable portfolio links can remain as published-work evidence.

Never invent a client incident, internal quote, private result, job title, personal memory, or developer-psychology statistic. When a first-person story would carry the argument and no evidence exists, put the question in the interview backlog. An empty chair is better than a fake witness.

## Narrative moves from the known to the possible

Where the subject has a meaningful before and after, begin with the workflow or assumption the reader already knows. Show what changed, then unfold the technical consequence until a better action becomes possible.

Do not force that arc onto a reference page, a timeless definition, or a subject with no real change. The story exists to clarify causality, not to satisfy a template.

Headings expose the argument. H2s carry the main decisions; H3s carry the steps or sub-decisions underneath them. A heading such as "Why this matters" announces importance without earning it.

## AI content still needs a human thesis

An AI engineering article may pass without revision when it has a defensible technical thesis, primary or reproducible evidence, honest limits, and a coherent reader path. Novelty is not a license to flatten it into a market summary.

Search pages with no useful disagreement enter the consolidation register. They stay published until evidence and explicit authorization support a redirect, merge, or removal.

## Titles and URLs

Titles should parse for a stranger and earn a click without lying. Short, speakable titles are preferred, but existing slugs do not move merely because a shorter phrase exists.

The audit records rename candidates. A rename ships only when the current title is clearly harmful and a direct redirect is prepared. `/articles/writing-ai-first-content/` remains the canonical URL for "Before You Ask an Expert."

## Release gate

Before publication, the owner must record:

- thesis and fair opposing/default view
- evidence provenance for material claims
- a heading-only read of the argument
- first-person count against the pre-edit baseline
- fixture, link, and strict-build results where relevant
- desktop and mobile renders
- canonical and redirect changes, including an explicit `none`
- reviewer findings and their resolution

The gate rewards judgment and proof. It must never reward aggressive adjectives, fake certainty, profanity count, or a boilerplate paragraph injected into every page.
