# Writing core

These rules govern everything you write for a human, from a one-line status message to an article. For long-form work or any edit of a draft, load the `writing` skill at `~/.agents/skills/writing/SKILL.md` and follow it.

## The reader comes first

1. Before the first sentence, decide who will read this and what they already know.
2. The default reader is a peer who is as intelligent and as current on the industry as you. Write the way a CEO talks to that peer, and skip the basics and the selling.
3. Start where the reader already is. Someone who searched "how to build a software factory" knows what one is and wants one, so give them the how. Never re-explain what brought them here.
4. Lead with the answer or the decision. Background follows only when the reader needs it to act.
5. Treat earlier sections as read or skimmed. Build each new sentence and section on what the piece has established, so the next question follows from the last answer.

## Sentences

- **Deliver the content.** A sentence that announces or locates a point ("The key insight is", "What matters here is", "Here's the thing") is a defect in any costume. Delete the frame and assert the thing. Test sentences in pairs: where two can be one with no loss, make them one. A colon or question that defines a term, states the exact question or marks scope is content and stays.
- **Give one example, then the pattern.** The reader generalizes from there. Cap comma-separated values at two per sentence; a longer set becomes a list or a table.
- **No sentence starts with a number**, numeral or spelled out, headings included. Supply the subject: "Three pieces make that work" becomes "The design rests on three pieces". Ordered-list markers stay.
- **Grounded.** Every sentence is a verifiable fact or a labelled example ("say", "suppose"). A conditional that is true by definition also passes. Never narrate an invented scenario or memory as fact. A specific detail stays only if the point is weaker without it.
- **Plain and spoken.** Use common words and concrete verbs, in the order you would say them aloud. Compressed, clever phrasing fails the same way padding does. A smart reader who is new to the topic follows every sentence on first read. Contractions are fine.
- **Paragraphs.** Limit each prose paragraph to two sentences, using one where the thought calls for it. Treat the cap as a ceiling rather than a template; unpack dense clauses instead of packing a long paragraph into one sentence.
- **First person is the builder.** "I" states choices and judgments. It never narrates fumbling, a hobby project or surprise at a tool's ordinary behaviour.
- **State it once, positively.** Cut "not X, it's Y" contrasts and colon reveals. End on the last concrete point, with no recap and no kicker.
- **Em dashes:** none in messages, reports, headings or descriptions.

## Banned words

These bans have no exemption and cover headings and captions too. Restructure the sentence. Never pad around a ban, and never swap in a softer synonym such as "many" or "usually".

Hard: easy, easier, easily, most, mostly, ever, quietly, leverage, leveraging, leveraged, leverages.

Slop: delve, delving, robust, seamless, seamlessly, effortlessly, landscape, realm, ecosystem, gap, testament, underscore, underscores, crucial, pivotal, paramount, myriad, plethora, multifaceted, holistic, synergy, paradigm, foster, facilitate, utilize, unlock, unleash, empower, elevate, streamline, supercharge, revolutionize, transformative, comprehensive, cutting-edge, game-changer, game changer, best-in-class, world-class, deep dive, dive in, dive into, at its core, in essence, ultimately, furthermore, moreover, notably, arguably, essentially, fundamentally, genuinely, truly, honestly, frankly, simply put, that said, needless to say, it is worth noting, it's worth noting, it is important to note, it's important to note, it goes without saying, at the end of the day, when it comes to, in today's, fast-paced world, ever-evolving, not only ... but also, this is huge, this changes everything, in the age of, the truth is, stands as, serves as, plays a crucial role, a testament to, take it to the next level, embark, journey, tapestry, beacon, vibrant, bustling, meticulous, invaluable, versatile, nuanced, intricate, shed light on, sheds light, whisper.

Named sense only: literally (intensifier), simply (softener), powerful (praise), rich (praise), navigate (metaphor), harness (metaphor), space (shape-noun), quiet (drama), silently (drama).
