# Reference and calibration

## Observations from the reference

Source: Cheryl Feldman, [The Best Thing I Built on Salesforce: My Career](https://cherfeldman.blogspot.com/2014/01/the-best-thing-i-built-on-salesforce-my.html), January 27, 2014.

The piece addresses one person directly. A recent conversation prompts a chronological account, then the ending returns to why the author wanted to share it. Concrete work tasks make the career changes legible. The author names embarrassment and excitement plainly, using dialogue, contractions, asides, and occasional exclamations. People who helped remain part of the account. The closing directs a personal outcome toward a reader facing a similar uncertainty.

These are observations about this article, not rules for everything the author writes. Comments and site furniture were excluded.

## Adaptation choices for this skill

Use those broad qualities to make original writing from the user's material. The letter format, retrospective frame, success arc, dialogue, and closing return are options. Keep the user's voice and actual outcomes. Avoid copying memorable phrases, the title construction, or personal history. The article supplies craft evidence, not claims about the user's life or current technology.

## Original illustration

The following is a fictional calibration fixture, not Ninad's experience. Its facts exist only to show how supplied notes can become prose. Never reuse them as autobiographical material.

**Illustrative notes:** A writer kept getting the same installation question. They answered it individually, then added the missing step to the guide. They were embarrassed that they had assumed the step was obvious. They have not measured whether the revision helped.

**Possible prose:**

I kept answering the same installation question, one person at a time. Eventually I added the missing step to the guide. I was embarrassed that I'd assumed it was obvious. It was obvious to me, and that had been enough for me to leave it out. I don't yet know whether the change helped.

The passage keeps a small event small. It names the supplied feeling and preserves the unmeasured result. The fixture is an illustration, not a structure to repeat.
