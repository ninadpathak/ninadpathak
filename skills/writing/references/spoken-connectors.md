## Rhythm notes

- A paragraph often runs: orienting opener, claim, "which means" consequence, concrete example, short punchy closer.
- Short sentences land the point after a long explanatory one. ("It can restart. It can survive crashes.")
- Mid-sentence connectors ("which", "so", "and the reason is") let one long sentence carry a chain of reasoning without breaking it up into disconnected statements.
- Setup and signposting entries ("Here is the idea", "Let's talk about", "Zooming out", "The key thing is", "Here's the thing", "So the takeaway is", "Now, ...", "Going back to ...") come from the source speaker and are part of the voice. Space them using the soft guidance in SKILL.md ("Setup sentences: use them, on a budget"): about one per 250 to 350 words of body, not stacked in one paragraph or in consecutive paragraphs, and not the same phrase twice. The relationship connectors (So, And, But, Which means, The reason is) are not budgeted.
- The banned-opener list in SKILL.md ("In", "This", "By", "Finally", "Most", "Ever", numerals) overrides this bank. Where a bank entry starts a sentence with a banned word, restructure it ("Finally, ..." becomes "And the last piece is ...").
- The word list in "Banned words and AI tells" (SKILL.md) also overrides this bank. Check any bridge you add against it.
